Machine learning models that fit the data but break the physics: how to build learnt models that respect conservation laws, boundary conditions, and other prior knowledge.

Call for Contributions {% if site.submission.url != "" %} Submit via CMT {% endif %}

About

Machine learning methods have been increasingly used to model, simulate, and forecast scientific and engineering systems due to their performance and lower computational costs. However, even when a model has a good fit to the data it can be scientifically wrong: creating or destroying mass and energy, producing negative concentrations, violating boundary conditions, or drifting into unphysical regimes over predictions outside the training time horizon. These violations compromise the trust in a learnt model, hindering its adoption in simulation pipelines, digital twins, or decision making.

The problem of incorporating prior knowledge or constraints into these methods has sparked interest in the literature, and different approaches exist: soft penalty terms, projection, correction and differentiable optimisation layers, and hard architectural guarantees. However, several limitations exist when choosing between them, at a cost in accuracy, training stability, and compute.

In this session, we promote discussion across machine learning methods, from new approaches to successful applications in real-world systems. The focus is on promoting transparency, trust, and security in machine learning methods.
